# Rev3 field regression and Rev4 repairs

The supplied client capture contains 132,482 events from 18:00:04 to 18:02:47 UTC on 2026-10-14; the server contains 100,182 events from 18:00:33 to 18:03:01 UTC. Both identify the Rev3 build. Fifteen account identities occur; published evidence exports aggregate numbers only. Client export happened after the session stopped. Rotating tails and unsynchronized clocks prevent exact test-to-trace/one-way timing reconstruction.

The screenshots show download/upload results of 23.2/4.27, 4.78/10.4 and 0.91/0.44 Mbps. They do not identify SINGLE/MULTI mode or timestamps. The user reports initialization failures, carrier crashes, and Telegram staying unusable after reconnect. Successful build gates and fresh-state laboratory speed tests did not establish field usability.

## Observed failure sequence

Thirteen DATA receive operations time out around 18:00:36 UTC, followed by TLS repair failures on all fifteen accounts around 18:00:37. Seven client carriers reconnect: one CONTROL_QUEUE_LIMIT, five CARRIER_REATTACH and one IO_OR_PROTOCOL_ERROR. Seven server carriers report TRANSPORT_TIMEOUT and reconnect. Client APPEND/cleanup operations stall for roughly 23–30 seconds during this interval. The captures show network capability notifications; they do not prove a change of network or a specific cause of the physical interruption.

Reconnect/ready events resume, but bulk ranges remain unacknowledged for up to 57 seconds on the client and 61 seconds on the server. The controller repeatedly reaches its 64 KiB minimum window. Server held DATA message counts commonly exceed 100 of the 192 profile slots. The client reports WALLET_HISTORY_MISSING 351 times; missing history must not become a zero-spend or cleanup proof. Socket EOF/reset alone is not interpreted as a transport crash.

## Reproduced implementation faults

1. The split DATA worker starts before carrier activation and coordinator bootstrap. Already queued envelopes on a replacement carrier enter process() before its activation gate is ready, causing SESSION_NOT_ACTIVATED and repeated carrier exits. The new interrupted-bulk/retained-state test reproduced that loop in the released Rev3 source. Rev4 starts the worker once, after both gates finish. A second direct reproduction showed that old fast envelopes in the control mailbox could cause the same error in mixed mode; those envelopes now stay in the existing bounded pending cache and replay after bootstrap, without registration, plaintext delivery or receipts before activation.
2. A retained logical actor answers application OPEN using its canceled original physical context. Even with a valid OPEN_OK received, select can discard the result and close the new application socket immediately. Trace reproduction showed OPEN_OK followed by client FIN within the same millisecond, then the caller timing out and retrying. Rev4 uses the logical session lifetime and rejects already canceled callers explicitly. A direct test repeats this answer 64 times after physical cancellation, and the application test checks real TCP after rejoin.
3. Client.Open selects/monitors a physical account, but the shared actor previously sent its OPEN through the actor's original account. Rev4 honors the stored selected carrier for OPEN, with fallback to authenticated surviving carriers. Logical stream ownership stays centralized.
4. Serial selection of two/four DATA mailboxes on one receive socket adds repeated mobile round trips without providing extra physical parallelism. Rev4 advertises one active mailbox for the one actual reader. Historical folders remain available to recovery/cleanup.
5. Fixed 800 ms bulk/650 ms short ACK budgets could sit below independently measured DATA/ACK propagation, repeatedly reducing usable service to 64 KiB. Rev4 bounds extra delay above authenticated low-flight/isolated-short calibration. Repaired samples cannot inflate the floor; collective stall/outage and existing memory/quota bounds remain. Actual application latency and throughput are independent checks.

6. After a 30 s provider blackout, the recovered traces showed carrier retry sleeping for 16–24 s even after the provider returned. The pending application transfer then exhausted its deadline despite reconnect progress. Rev4 retains bounded retry (at most 3 s including jitter) for previously authenticated carriers during the existing 90 s logical survival interval. Cold/prolonged failures keep exponential pacing; global login concurrency and immediate-retry cooldown are unchanged.

7. Repeated long-blackout tests exposed a second failure: the original message socket recovered, but all eight pending bulk exchanges exceeded their deadline in one run. Successfully repaired ranges still fed their entire outage residence into congestion feedback. Rev4 measures the new physical turn only after an authenticated NACK repair definitively republishes the complete remaining range. Original residence remains in the oldest-flight diagnostic; unknown/partial repair does not reset it, and no flight or quota debt is refunded. A stalled repaired range still triggers the normal outage guard.

8. A focused scheduler test reproduced a valid authenticated NACK authorizing repair while the same item remained parked on a stale delivery-admission wake. That wake could depend on an ACK blocked by the missing range. Rev4 clears that obsolete wake only for authorized repairs of the NACK stream. The physical adapter then rechecks visibility grace, the bounded repair slot and quota. Duplicate NACKs cannot bypass active writer ownership. This changes scheduling, not repair permission or accounting.

9. Delayed-BODY/reader-restart runs exposed shared-window head-of-line blocking without a carrier crash. A delayed BODY held 192 KiB while the controller shrank its shared window to 64–98 KiB; 84 queued items on healthy streams remained parked. Rev4 permits one in-order DATA frame (at most 32 KiB) on an empty bulk stream to discover/continue its path. That frame stays charged until authenticated progress or retirement; writer completion cannot replenish it. The 16 MiB session ceiling, per-stream flow credit, actor limits and all physical journal/wallet quotas remain enforced. A direct reproduction verifies that one stream can progress, a second unacknowledged frame cannot pass, old flight is retained, and the hard ceiling still blocks admission.

10. A repeated delayed-visibility run failed a 4 KiB message after its request and reply both encountered replication delay. Bulk visibility/recovery pacing applied to the short exchange, leaving the reply invisible past the unchanged 3 s application deadline. Rev4 gives pending short DATA early STATUS discovery every 400 ms for at most 4 s, then retains the normal interval. A timer cannot authorize DATA: a valid NACK and the existing bounded repair permit remain required. Short copies use a bounded 250–650 ms visibility grace; bulk keeps its established pacing. Tests verify prompt authenticated short recovery, unchanged bulk grace and unchanged charged bytes.

11. The controller also treated fresh useful ACKs as a reason to reduce the whole session whenever an old visibility tail exceeded the age target. This repeatedly drove healthy streams to a 64 KiB window despite responsive short traffic. Rev4 reduces for short-path queue pressure or a complete feedback turn without useful progress. A collective three-second progress stall still enters OUTAGE. Fresh ACKs do not by themselves classify a delayed replica as global congestion; hard flight and journal bounds remain unchanged. The direct test failed before this change and also checks that an actual progress stall still reduces flight.

12. The complete delayed-visibility build then reproduced WALLET_STATE and carrier reattach while every application transfer completed. Trace showed grant 18 arriving before previously unseen grant 14. A single high-water mark incorrectly treated the delayed offer as retired authority. Rev4 accepts unseen current-session offers out of order and atomically records retired-offer tombstones. The 4,096-record bound advances a conservative floor when pruning; a new session begins above its durable historical high-water mark, including old wallet files without tombstones. Retired/fenced offers are not queued, duplicate offers never renew usage, and no unknown history becomes zero-spend proof. Focused tests cover reordering, retirement replay, restart history and bounded pruning.

13. Fresh performance qualification exposed a downstream tail below the 90 Mbps floor. Profiling measured 73 GiB of allocations over a 90-second diagnostic; queued DATA copies accounted for 7 GiB, and sorting all pending work consumed about 12.5 CPU seconds. Rev4 schedules only runnable items while retaining every in-flight range for accounting/recovery, and sizes DATA from validated immutable metadata without copying queued plaintext. The worker/queue/physical limits remain bounded. Final speed/RTT evidence is required independently.

## Comparison and limits

The new retained-state mobile HTTP scenario fails its first twelve-download phase in both the recovered Rev2 and the Rev3-based model before the layout/queue-budget rework: requests time out at the unchanged 15-second limit. One mailbox alone also fails. The repaired transport completes GET/POST/GET with byte verification and short-message requests during every phase; final repeated evidence is recorded separately. This model comparison does not claim that Rev2 and Rev3 perform equally on the user's actual network.

The field captures reveal symptoms and recovery state; they do not contain the exact installed journal/wallet files or calibrated provider wire delays. Test parameters such as retained allocations, radio delay and command costs are declared stress inputs, not a decomposition of recorded wrapper durations. No raw log, account mapping, private key or payload is published. Numeric evidence is in `evidence/rev3_field_failure_summary.json`.
