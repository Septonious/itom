# Targeted RC11/RC12 comparison

Same ten-account 10 ms/500 Mbps client and 20 ms/1 Gbps server reference. The phased test retains 7.5 MiB/150 old remote ordinals per account, adds the observed 20 ms durable-commit service time after startup (real fsync remains enabled), verifies every download/upload byte and carries persistent 32-byte requests during both phases. This is a targeted service-cost replay, not full qualification or an eight-core CPU emulation.

A 1 MiB/stream preliminary pair rejected the larger message cushion: candidate download 23.508 versus RC11 28.340 Mbps; upload 31.485 versus 31.054; short-request mean 266 versus 210 ms. A longer 4 MiB/stream pair with a smaller cushion improved throughput (51.206/32.920 versus 46.440/28.157 Mbps) but still raised short-request mean/max (285/1,100 versus 248/844 ms). These are single-run samples; the tuning is rejected conservatively rather than claiming a win from throughput alone.

The current candidate reserves just one message slot for the next short request, while retaining the smaller recovery fast byte lease and stable refill target. Its regression also exercises real partial physical deletion proofs: available message capacity must restore a multi-message grant rather than repeatedly divide the free remainder down to one. Every spent byte/ordinal remains charged until its exact proof. The minimal one-slot/128 KiB experiment also regressed download and short requests (37.775/35.833 Mbps, short mean/max 336/1,042 ms), and is rejected. The next candidate addresses the scheduling cause: a fully message-spent fast grant no longer counts as usable while its separate fence is in flight. Replacement permissions still require actual journal headroom; the old grant is neither closed nor refunded. Immutable issued limits are cached to avoid adding a journal read to each report. Recovery fast leases use half a maximum bulk BODY (256 KiB) and preserve two refill ordinals. The strengthened regression fails without the proactive replacement and verifies the old grant is still open and fully charged when the new permission appears. Results are pending.
